Car-Sharing or Owning: Price the Whole Transport Route

Decision rule: Compare what your household would actually spend with shared cars, rides and public transport against the full cost of one owned car over the same years. Then test whether the shared-car route covers the trips where timing matters. A low hourly rate or a low instalment does not answer either question.

Model the car route →Price a single trip

LTA describes car-sharing as short-period personal rental, commonly booked through an app with return to the original or a designated location. Charges depend on vehicle, time and distance. Check a real vehicle and trip in the operator's current app; the figures below are hypothetical arithmetic, not market averages.

The middle option is a route, not a car substitute for every trip

Shared cars can cover journeys that need a vehicle without paying to hold one every day. But the rest of the household's travel does not vanish: add ride-hailing, bus and rail, and any weekend rental that remains. Count journeys for every household member. One shared booking also cannot serve two people who need a car in different places at the same time.

Where car-sharing can fit

It is easiest to test when car-type journeys are planned, the pickup point is practical, the vehicle can be returned on time, and another mode covers a failed booking. An occasional multi-stop day may suit a shared car better than several separate rides; compare those quotes per trip. Preserve a record of completed bookings for 8–12 weeks before extrapolating.

Where the route starts to fail

Daily exact-time journeys can turn booking, pickup, return and extension into recurring work. A vehicle may not be available at the needed location or size. Check whether child seats, mobility needs, an additional driver, a cross-border trip or a longer return window are permitted and priced by the chosen operator. A car that is unavailable for a school or care trip is not equivalent to one waiting at home.

Compare five-year totals, then find the sharing threshold

Use the owned-car example from the full route guide: S$120,000 purchase less S$50,000 net exit, S$47,500 running costs, S$5,000 interest and S$6,000 rides still needed with the car. That car route totals S$128,500 over five years. For an invented alternative household plan, suppose shared-car bookings cost S$900 a month, other rides S$300, and public transport S$150.

That is a threshold for this example's sharing bill, not a general Singapore price. A S$10,000 lower car exit would raise the threshold by about S$167 a month.

Build booking totals from the vehicle's current quoted time, distance and platform charges. One operator's rate page illustrates why a headline hourly charge alone is incomplete; verify its current inclusions, optional cover, parking and ERP for your chosen booking. Keep accident excess as a separate cash-risk check, not a routine fee added to every trip.

Give reliability its own test

Write down the trips where a missed or delayed booking would materially disrupt work, school or care. For each, check the nearest appropriate vehicle at the actual day and time, pickup walk, return location, booking extension, and the cost of a realistic backup ride. If a backup works, include its expected cost in the shared route. If it does not, mark the trip as an access failure; do not invent a dollar value to make it disappear.

Run a short household trial

  1. Log every car-type journey, including weekday errands and trips by another adult.
  2. Price the correct vehicle size and complete booking window, including driving time, parked time and return.
  3. Record whether the booking was available and whether the fallback worked.
  4. Compare the actual monthly total with the car route using a conservative net sale value.

Use two gates before buying

Cost gate: Does the complete shared route still cost less under a busy month and a realistic car exit? Access gate: Can it deliver the recurring trips that matter, with a viable fallback? If the first passes and the second fails, try a different pickup location, reserved rental or trip schedule before assuming ownership is the only fix. If ownership appears cheaper, run the cash-flow test as well.

FAQ

Is car-sharing cheaper than ownership?

There is no universal answer. Compare full route totals for your booking pattern and a vehicle-specific exit value.

Should I add fuel to every shared-car quote?

Only if the chosen booking terms make you pay it separately. Check time, distance, platform, fuel or charging, parking and ERP without double counting included items.

What about accident excess?

Record the contract's excess and available cover as downside cash exposure. Do not treat the full excess as an ordinary cost on every booking.
