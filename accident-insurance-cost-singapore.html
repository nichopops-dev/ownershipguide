Personal Accident Insurance Cost in Singapore: Prices and Cover

Published annual examples checked on 20 September 2026 include S$196.20 for Sompo PAStar Deluxe, occupation Class 1, and S$233.26 for Income PA Assurance Basic without infectious-disease cover, both including GST. These are named product examples, not a market-wide price range or a personal quote. Eligibility, occupation and benefits differ.

Quick answer

Compare the annual premium against the specific loss you want covered: accidental treatment costs, a scheduled disability payout or limited cash support. Read the accident definition and benefit limits first. A large accidental-death sum does little for an outpatient bill if the relevant medical benefit is small or excluded.

The LIA–GIA accident and health disclosure guidelines distinguish reimbursement, continuing income and lump-sum benefits. Use the product summary to identify the type, then check the policy wording for definitions, time limits, deductibles and exclusions.

Worked example: a treatment sub-limit

Assume an illustrative policy has S$2,000 available medical reimbursement per accident, but physiotherapy is limited to S$300. An eligible accident leads to S$600 of other covered treatment plus S$500 of physiotherapy.

  • Total bills: S$600 + S$500 = S$1,100.
  • Illustrative reimbursement: S$600 + min(S$500, S$300) = S$900.
  • Amount retained by the household: S$1,100 − S$900 = S$200.

This assumes no excess, no previous use of the limit, no competing reimbursement and compliance with all claim conditions. It is not a calculation for either named product above. The point is that a large unused overall limit does not remove a treatment sub-limit.

Check the accident definition against your actual activity

Do not use “I was injured” as a substitute for the policy’s definition. For example, Income describes an external, sudden and unexpected event and excludes certain internal injuries and strains. Ask about the particular risk you want covered: cycling, motorcycle commuting, delivery work, contact sport, exercise injuries or travel. The answer can differ by activity, occupational use and policy.

If infectious-disease protection is offered, check the named conditions, relevant benefit sections, waiting period and exclusions. It does not turn the accident policy into cover for every illness.

Where it fits with your existing cover

  • Medical-bill protection: establish what your hospital and employer plans pay, including outpatient exclusions and what ends when employment changes.
  • Income continuity: test an absence from work caused by illness as well as accidental injury. An accident-only payment cannot be assumed to cover both.
  • Dependants: calculate the support gap if an earner dies. An accidental-death benefit is conditional on cause; review broader life cover separately.
  • Cash: keep enough accessible money for immediate expenses and claim processing. Record which costs would remain uninsured.

Use hospitalisation versus accident insurance, accident versus disability-income insurance and accident versus term life for those separate comparisons.

A quote checklist you can reuse

  1. State your occupation and daily duties accurately; ask what changes must be reported.
  2. List the activities and countries relevant to you. Ask the insurer to point to the applicable clause.
  3. Record medical reimbursement, physiotherapy and other treatment limits separately.
  4. Check the disability compensation schedule, any age reductions and renewal conditions.
  5. For cash benefits, check waiting periods, maximum duration and any income cap.
  6. List existing cover and ask how overlapping expense claims are coordinated.
  7. Compare annual cash outlay, optional benefits and future renewal affordability.

For a household illustration, two S$240 annual policies cost S$480 a year or S$40 a month as a budgeting equivalent. Over five years that is S$2,400 if prices do not change. That figure helps you weigh the commitment against the identified gap; it is not an expected claim value or a reason to buy cover by itself.

Prepare for a claim before you need one

Keep the schedule, wording, insurer contact and employer benefits summary together. After an accident, follow the insurer’s notification and treatment deadlines, and retain bills and an accurate account of the event. Ask before assuming a referral, practitioner, follow-up treatment or piece of equipment qualifies. Never assume repeated reimbursement of the same expense is permitted.

Frequently asked questions

Is the lowest advertised personal-accident premium available to everyone?

No. Check the plan, occupation class, eligibility, optional benefits and payment frequency behind the price. Ask for a quote using your actual circumstances.

Does a large accidental-death benefit pay my treatment bills?

Medical reimbursement is a separate benefit with its own limits. Check that section rather than using the accidental-death headline as a treatment budget.

Can personal-accident insurance replace broad medical or income protection?

It should not be assumed to do so. Compare the covered causes and claim conditions against illness, injury, treatment and inability to work separately.

How do I compare monthly and yearly premiums?

Multiply the quoted monthly payment by 12 and compare it with the quoted annual premium for the same cover. An annual premium divided by 12 is only a budgeting equivalent.
