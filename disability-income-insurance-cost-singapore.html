Disability Income Insurance Cost Singapore: Compare Premiums and Claim Cash

Compare the cost of disability income insurance only after matching what the policy pays and when. A cheaper annual premium can leave a larger waiting-period cash need, a shorter benefit term or a different occupational test. In the selected comparison below, S$180 lower annual premium comes with S$9,000 less first-year benefit in a qualifying twelve-month interruption. That is conditional scenario arithmetic, not an expected return.

Match the benefit and conditions before comparing price

Obtain dated quotes for the same applicant, occupation and duties, disclosed medical history, monthly benefit, disability definition, deferred period, payment duration and expiry age. Check recovery rules, other-income adjustments, renewal terms, exclusions, premium loading and any waiver. Do not compare a promotional entry price with an undiscounted renewal or assume optional membership is free.

MoneySense explains that disability-income benefits depend on qualifying inability to work and can leave part of earnings unreplaced. An occupation definition, rather than a diagnostic label alone, governs the claim. Hospitalisation, life/TPD and critical illness benefits use other tests. Use the monthly-gap sizing worksheet before choosing a benefit amount.

A named design example is AIA Pay Protector: its current product page distinguishes cover to age 65 from monthly payouts for up to five years. Its notes also say entry-age-based premium rates are not guaranteed against future revision. A maximum payment period is not the same as the age to which a policy can remain in force. This example establishes those distinctions, not a market price or product recommendation.

Separate annual premium, instalments and monthly provision

These are invented quote terms, not AIA or other insurer offers. Quote A pays a selected S$3,000 per budget month after a 90-day deferral; quote B pays the same amount after 180 days. Assume all other relevant definitions, adjustments, payment-duration limits and renewal terms match for this exercise, and that each covers the modelled period. Actual quotes may differ on several terms.

Calculate a qualifying first-year interruption

Use twelve equal 30-day budget months. Main-earner income stops; the partner's S$1,500 contribution continues and arrives before bills. Goals pause. Essential payments remain S$4,800, giving a S$3,300 monthly cash gap. The selected policy is already in force, disability qualifies continuously and the stated benefit is the amount payable after all contract adjustments. No other replacement income or one-off cost is assumed.

A's 90-day deferral occupies the first three model months; payments arrive at the ends of months four through twelve. B's 180-day deferral occupies six model months; payments arrive at the ends of months seven through twelve. Actual calendars, eligibility and receipt dates must replace this simplified schedule.

Test receipt delays and declined claims

Both policies leave S$300 monthly uncovered once the selected S$3,000 benefit pays against the S$3,300 gap. A's first three-month gap is S$9,900, but bills through month four require S$13,200 before its first end-of-month receipt. Peak draw occurs just before a later receipt: S$15,600 for A and S$24,600 for B. A model based only on the deferral length misses that payment timing.

If all nine A benefits are received retrospectively only at the end of month twelve, the initial S$39,600 gap must be funded first. A S$30,000 reserve is S$9,600 short before payment, even though it would finish with S$17,400 after the assumed S$27,000 receipt. If no claim qualifies, that S$9,600 remains a shortfall. Retrospective payment is an explicit stress assumption, not a promise.

Check paid leave, employer protection and household support for their actual amounts and end dates. Treat reimbursements or benefits as receipts under stated conditions, not as cash already in the bank. Separate medical, care and known annual bills from this income-gap reserve, then test a combined event.

Choose sustainable terms rather than the smallest premium

A written quote should show what makes its price different. Distinguish the policy's in-force period, maximum payments for a claim, any aggregate limit and the tests that can change after time or recovery. For a five-year maximum at S$3,000 monthly, S$180,000 is the nominal sum of sixty hypothetical payments, not upfront money or a promise of protection for every year to retirement.

Ask how premiums may change and whether a waiver applies; none is assumed in these quote budgets. Disclose actual work and health information and confirm replacement acceptance, exclusions and commencement before cancelling existing cover. FAQ

Is there one typical disability-income premium for Singapore?

A useful price comparison needs dated quotes matched to the applicant, occupation, medical disclosures, benefit and contract terms. The S$720 and S$540 examples here are hypothetical, not market rates.

Is annual premium divided by twelve my monthly instalment?

No. It is a budgeting equivalent. A selected S$720 annual premium needs S$60 monthly provision, while a separate S$65 monthly-payment option costs S$780 over twelve payments.

Does a shorter deferred period eliminate the need for cash?

No. Bills can fall due before the first receipt, benefits can leave a residual monthly gap, and claim assessment can add delay. Test the actual payment sequence and a no-benefit case.

Does cover to age 65 mean every claim pays until age 65?

Check both the in-force expiry age and the maximum claim payment period. They can differ. A five-year payment limit is not thirty years of income replacement.
