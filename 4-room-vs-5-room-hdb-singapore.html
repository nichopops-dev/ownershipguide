4-Room vs 5-Room HDB: Layout, Space and Cost

A standard 5-room HDB flat does not automatically give you an extra bedroom. HDB lists three bedrooms for both 4-room and 5-room flats. The practical decision is whether the specific 5-room layout gives enough useful extra space to justify its price, running costs and any location trade-off.

Quick answer

Choose between actual floor plans and household routines. A well-planned 4-room can suit a family that uses three bedrooms effectively. A 5-room can be worth more for a larger common area or study zone. If you need four enclosed bedrooms, verify that requirement on the plan instead of relying on the room label.

HDB’s flat-type guide lists a suggested study among 5-room features and notes that layouts and floor areas vary. Its descriptions are not a substitute for a particular resale flat’s plan.

For a concrete project example, HDB’s Lakeside View information lists approximate internal areas of 90 m² for a 4-room and 110 m² for a 5-room, with stated floor areas of 93 m² and 113 m² including the air-con ledge. Those project figures are not universal sizes. Compare internal area with internal area, and inspect where the additional space sits.

Map people and routines before allocating rooms

Start with sleeping arrangements, then add work, study, meals, storage and overnight care. Give each recurring activity a place at the time it actually happens. A desk beside the dining table may work for occasional admin but fail when two people need quiet calls during dinner preparation.

The larger flat requires S$33,600 more outside the loan including BSD: an extra S$30,000 toward the price and S$3,600 of duty. It also adds S$90,000 to the mortgage.

At an assumed 3.5% annual loan rate over 25 years, the extra S$90,000 adds about S$450.56 a month. That payment contains both principal and interest; it is a budget commitment, not all a non-recoverable cost. If rates, tenure, valuation or loan eligibility differ, recalculate.

BSD uses IRAS’s current residential bands. The two flats may also differ in renovation work, service and conservancy charges, tax and repairs. Obtain those figures instead of assuming every expense rises in proportion to floor area. The S$33,600 is not necessarily all cash: cash and CPF eligibility must be checked separately.

Use your HDB Flat Eligibility letter for the assessed purchase, grant and HDB-loan position; obtain a bank assessment where relevant. A grant or a loan is not established by choosing a room type.

Compare the extra space with the alternative use of the money

Describe what S$450.56 monthly and S$33,600 upfront would otherwise do for this household: preserve a reserve, meet childcare bills, support parents or reduce borrowing. Then describe the concrete inconvenience the larger home removes. “More spacious” is less useful than “both adults can work while the children sleep without using a bedroom as an office”.

Price the alternative that solves the same problem in a 4-room, such as different furniture or improved storage. It may be cheaper, but it cannot create daylight, an additional bathroom or a feasible enclosed room where the structure does not allow one. Do not spend heavily on a workaround that still leaves the daily routine unworkable.

Compare location and condition on the same shortlist

A larger flat farther away can add travel time or paid care. A smaller flat in better condition can avoid renovation and temporary accommodation. Compare remaining lease, floor, noise, accessibility and the route to work or school alongside size.

  1. Obtain the floor plan and confirm which changes are existing and approved.
  2. Measure the furniture and circulation space in each room.
  3. Simulate a weekday morning, simultaneous work/study and a visiting parent.
  4. Price required repairs and renovation with comparable scopes.
  5. Check total upfront funding, monthly costs and remaining reserves.
  6. Repeat the comparison with the likely household five years from now.

Buy for a plausible next stage

Keep a likely child or caregiving change in the plan, but distinguish it from a remote possibility. Review current needs versus one stage ahead, a bigger home before children and living near parents versus school. Combine the baby budget and longer-term child costs with the mortgage.

Frequently asked questions

Does a 5-room HDB flat have four bedrooms?

Not automatically. HDB’s standard descriptions list three bedrooms for both 4-room and 5-room flats. Check the actual floor plan and approved layout.

Is a 4-room flat always 90 square metres?

No. Areas and layouts vary. Check the specific project or resale plan and whether the quoted area includes an air-con ledge.

When is a 5-room flat worth the higher price?

When its actual additional space solves a recurring household need and the complete purchase and running budget remains workable.

Should I compare only the monthly mortgage difference?

No. Also compare upfront funding, stamp duty, renovation, ongoing charges, location costs and the reserves left after moving.
