Critical Illness vs Disability Income Singapore: Compare Triggers and Cash

Critical illness and disability income insurance answer different claim questions. A covered illness can trigger a lump sum without establishing inability to work; a qualifying disability can stop earnings without meeting a named critical illness definition. Compare the losses, eligibility and cash timing rather than treating one benefit as a cheaper version of the other.

Read two independent sets of conditions

MoneySense describes critical illness cover as cash for an illness or surgery meeting the stated definition. The covered stage, waiting conditions and policy limits matter; some plans have earlier-stage or multiple-payment features. A diagnosis alone does not establish every policy's benefit. Check any applicable survival condition and whether a claim reduces or ends other cover.

Disability income uses the actual inability-to-work and occupation terms, deferred period, recovery conditions and payable benefit. It does not automatically pay because a critical illness benefit has been accepted. Neither policy replaces eligible medical-expense insurance, and severe-disability care and life/TPD cover have other tests.

Use the same needs and starting cash for every option

This independent illustration uses the sizing worksheet's S$4,800 essentials and continuing S$1,500 partner contribution. The main earner receives no earnings for twelve equal 30-day model months. The monthly gap is S$3,300 and the yearly gap S$39,600. Add S$6,000 one-off recovery and medical cash at the start, after other medical cover; it is outside essentials. Total event payments net of partner support are S$45,600.

All options begin with the same S$20,000 accessible cash before new premiums. Other earmarked bills and existing insurance are already funded. Selected annual new premiums are S$900 for CI and S$720 for DI, paid at the start; none renews inside the 360-day model. These are invented prices. Both premiums total S$1,620, leaving S$18,380 to front the event.

In the qualifying CI case, a selected S$30,000 lump sum arrives at the end of month two, after all its conditions are satisfied. In the qualifying DI case, a selected S$3,000 arrives at each month-end from month four through twelve after a 90-day model deferral, totalling S$27,000. The both-cover case assumes both contracts qualify and neither reduces these receipts. No payout or processing time is promised.

Check the cash low point before looking at year-end

Before the CI receipt at the end of month two, the household must pay the S$6,000 one-off amount plus two S$3,300 gaps: S$12,600. With CI-only premium included, cumulative required cash is S$13,500, leaving S$6,500 before that receipt. In the both-cover case, the corresponding peak need is S$14,220 including both premiums, leaving S$5,780.

DI-only's largest pre-receipt event draw is S$21,600, just before the month-twelve benefit. Add its S$720 upfront premium: required starting cash is S$22,320. S$20,000 is therefore S$2,320 short at that point, even though the year-end table shows S$680 after the final assumed S$3,000 receipt. The DI-only sequence needs more opening liquidity or a separately verified payment arrangement; final cash does not cure the earlier deadline.

If no selected benefit qualifies, only premiums and spending remain. If a payment is delayed, redraw the dated ledger rather than treating its original date as guaranteed. A CI lump sum is finite: retaining S$6,000 for one-off needs leaves S$24,000, about 7.27 months of the selected S$3,300 gap, before other costs. It is not an unlimited substitute for monthly income.

Review the loss that remains uncovered and fundable

A household may need cash for diagnosis-related choices and a long earnings interruption at the same time. Review each gap, existing medical and employer cover, actual eligibility and benefit duration. Do not use a universal CI-first or DI-first order, and do not delay an important review on the assumption that later underwriting is assured.

Annual premiums S$900 and S$720 are S$75 and S$60 monthly budgeting equivalents, S$135 together. Normal take-home pay S$5,400 plus partner support S$1,500, less essentials S$4,800 and a separate S$600 goal, leaves S$1,500 before these additions; both would leave S$1,365. Upfront payment dates, renewal affordability, expiry, aggregate benefit limits and the reserve low point still need checking. Confirm replacement terms before cancelling existing cover.

FAQ

Does a critical illness diagnosis automatically trigger disability income?

No. The illness benefit and the disability-income occupational test are separate. Each contract must qualify, including its relevant waiting, definition and payment conditions.

Can both fixed benefits pay for the same event?

They may under their actual terms, but do not assume two full payments. Check independent qualification, offsets, accelerated benefits and cover reduced by a claim.

Does positive year-end cash show the plan is fundable?

Not by itself. The selected DI-only case ends with S$680 but needs S$22,320 before a receipt, S$2,320 above its S$20,000 opening cash. Check the lowest balance by date.

Which policy should I buy first?

There is no universal order. Review the financial loss, existing cover, actual claim conditions, sustainable premiums and cash needed before benefits. These invented scenarios are not product recommendations.
