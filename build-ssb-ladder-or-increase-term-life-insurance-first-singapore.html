Build a Singapore Savings Bond Ladder or Increase Term Life Insurance First in Singapore (2026): Which Layer Should the Next Dollar Strengthen?

If people depend on your income, compare the protection they would have if you died with the money they would need. If that gap is already covered, decide whether your next savings should build a conservative reserve. These are different jobs for the same limited monthly surplus.

Decision snapshot

  • Start with the household's essential costs, outstanding obligations, existing insurance, and assets available to dependants.
  • Get an actual quote for any protection gap. Check the benefit, term, exclusions, and affordability before treating the gap as covered.
  • Keep immediate emergency cash separate from money earmarked for an SSB reserve.
  • Allocate the remaining surplus to a defined goal, with an amount and a date.

Term insurance does not replace all lost income in every circumstance. Job loss, disability, medical bills, and death are different risks. This comparison focuses on dependants' needs after death; review other gaps separately. See term life versus disability income insurance.

A worked protection-gap example

Illustrative household only: one parent wants to provide a three-year transition period for dependants. Essential monthly spending is S$2,500–S$3,500, excluding mortgage repayments because the example separately allows for clearing the outstanding mortgage. The household has S$100,000 of relevant existing cover and S$30,000 of assets specifically available for this purpose, after reserving emergency money.

The arithmetic is spending × months + obligations − relevant cover − available assets. This is a starting calculation, not a recommended sum assured. It excludes inflation, education funding, funeral costs, and any longer support period. Extending the example from three to five years adds S$60,000–S$84,000 of spending, taking the illustrative gap to S$220,000–S$280,000. A working spouse's ongoing income could reduce it; additional care needs could increase it.

Putting S$500 a month into savings adds S$6,000 in a year before interest and fees. That contribution is useful, but it does not close a S$160,000–S$196,000 protection gap within that year. Use the life insurance calculator to enter your own obligations and avoid counting the same expense twice.

How to allocate a limited monthly surplus

Suppose the household has S$500 a month available after essential spending and current commitments. It provisionally reserves S$60 for additional premiums. That S$60 is a budget allowance, not a quoted price or a promise of any amount of cover. If an acceptable policy fits that allowance, the remaining S$440 can accumulate toward the conservative reserve. Check actual SSB application amounts and timing before investing it.

If the required policy costs more, or cover is unavailable on acceptable terms, revise the plan. Do not assume a premium budget makes the risk disappear. Alternatives may include a different benefit or term, lower obligations, and a larger savings target, assessed against what dependants would still lack.

If there is no material death-protection gap, start with the reserve's purpose. A S$12,000 target funded at S$500 a month takes 24 months before interest and fees. If the spending date is 12 months away, the contribution would need to be S$1,000 a month on the same simplified basis. That timing gap is more useful to identify than an unspecified desire to hold more bonds.

When the priority changes

  • No financial dependants: the need for additional death cover may be limited. Check shared debts and other obligations before assigning the surplus to reserves.
  • Existing cover and assets meet the need: a defined conservative reserve may deserve the next contribution. Check that the policy term covers the years of dependency.
  • Immediate cash is thin: preserve enough accessible cash for urgent bills and premiums. See emergency cash versus term cover.
  • Family circumstances change: a child, mortgage, separation, or new parental-support obligation can change both the amount of cover needed and the reserve target.

Checks before acting

List current policies rather than relying on a remembered total. Check which person is covered, when the cover ends, and what the benefit actually pays. Keep retirement assets, emergency cash, and money already earmarked for other obligations out of the available-assets figure unless you deliberately choose to reassign them.

For SSBs, use MAS's Singapore Savings Bonds information to verify the current terms. This example assumes no investment return and makes no claim about the latest interest rate. For the wider savings sequence, read the SSB guide and saving more versus buying more insurance.

FAQ

When should term life insurance come before building an SSB ladder?

When dependants rely on your income and the current cover would leave the household exposed if you died too early.

When can building an SSB ladder come first?

When core protection is already adequate and the more immediate weakness is the lack of a medium-term reserve between instant cash and long-horizon risk assets.

Is an SSB ladder a substitute for term life insurance?

No. An SSB ladder is capital you slowly build. Term life insurance is immediate risk transfer for a low-probability but catastrophic event.

What is the cleanest deciding question?

Ask whether the next dollar needs to protect people from your death now, or whether it needs to strengthen conservative liquidity for known future use.
