Consignment or Dealer Sale: Put a Time Limit on the Higher Target

A dealer sale offers a committed exit price and fast completion. Consignment may pursue a higher retail price, but the owner keeps carrying the car while it remains unsold and pays the agreed fee. Compare the payout after costs, then calculate how long the target can take before the dealer offer wins.

Ask for two different numbers

A dealer offer is a price to buy the car. A consignment target is not a payout promise. Ask for the minimum price you must approve, every fee, and the exact calculation and timing of the amount paid to you.

Worked example: higher target, slower exit

Assume an immediate dealer purchase at S$46,000 with no separate old-car fee. The consignment target is S$51,000, the consignment fee is S$1,500, and the owner continues to bear S$900 a month of insurance, road tax, parking, finance and expected depreciation.

Illustrative figures; the consignment target is assumed to be achieved
Hypothetical outcomeDealer saleConsignment
Gross price or targetS$46,000S$51,000
Consignment or route feeS$0−S$1,500
Holding cost at two monthsS$0−S$1,800
Net payout at two monthsS$46,000S$47,700
Difference—S$1,700 more

At two months, consignment is ahead by S$1,700. At four months, net payout becomes S$45,900: S$51,000 − S$1,500 − S$3,600. The dealer route is then S$100 higher, before counting any extra repair, inspection or transport costs.

If the consignment price is reduced to S$49,000 after two months, its net payout becomes S$45,700. The original target should not continue to anchor the comparison after buyers reject it.

Calculate the break-even time

Use (consignment target − fee − dealer offer) ÷ monthly holding cost. With the example figures, (S$51,000 − S$1,500 − S$46,000) ÷ S$900 = 3.89 months. If the car has not sold at the target by then, the assumed consignment premium has been consumed.

This is a planning boundary, not a forecast. Set earlier review dates because the target price, condition and dealer offer can all change. Refresh the dealer offer rather than assuming it remains open for 3.89 months.

What the written consignment agreement should answer

Commercial points to make explicit before giving custody of the vehicle
TermQuestion to answer
Price authorityWhat is the asking price, minimum acceptable price, and who may approve a reduction?
Fees and expensesWhat fee is payable on sale, withdrawal or expiry, and what other costs can be deducted?
Custody and useWhere is the car kept, who may drive it, and how are test drives recorded?
Insurance, road tax and finesWho keeps cover in force and who bears incidents, excesses, tolls or penalties?
Duration and exitWhat is the term, notice period and process for collecting the car?
Buyer funds and owner payoutWho receives the money, where it is held, and when is the documented net amount paid?
Loan and transferWho obtains the settlement quote and how are clearance, transfer and handover sequenced?

Read the final agreement rather than relying on a verbal description. Photograph the condition and inventory items at handover, keep the keys and document trail clear, and record mileage when custody changes.

Keep payment and ownership milestones visible

LTA recommends written agreements, official receipts and payments to a dealer’s company identified by UEN. It also advises owners not to share Singpass credentials. Ownership should be transferred through the official process and physical handover coordinated with payment and buyer acceptance. LTA transaction and ownership-transfer safeguards.

The Ministry of Transport encourages consumers using motor dealers to consider CaseTrust-SVTA accredited businesses, which are assessed for matters including fair practices, transparency and dispute-resolution arrangements. Accreditation does not replace reading the agreement or verifying the transaction. Ministry of Transport guidance on dealer safeguards.

Dealer sale checks

  • Confirm whether the offer is firm or subject to inspection and when it expires.
  • List fees, loan settlement and residual proceeds separately.
  • Record when cleared funds are due and when the dealer takes physical possession.
  • Verify official ownership transfer rather than treating a signed sales form as completion.

For an outstanding loan, use the settlement and cash-gap worksheet. If you are choosing between trade-in and an owner-managed sale, use the net-proceeds comparison.

Decision rule

Consignment deserves consideration when the written expected payout remains meaningfully above an executable dealer offer after fees and a realistic sale period, and when the custody and payment terms are acceptable. An immediate dealer sale can be better when the premium is small, the break-even period is short or certainty and timing are valuable.

Frequently asked questions

Is the consignment asking price the amount I will receive?

No. Subtract the consignment fee, other agreed deductions and the cost of holding the car until sale. The final buyer price may also differ from the target.

How do I calculate the consignment break-even time?

Subtract the fee and immediate dealer offer from the consignment target, then divide the remaining premium by monthly holding cost. Refresh all offers as they expire.

What should be in a consignment agreement?

Record price authority, all fees, custody and test-drive rules, insurance responsibilities, duration and exit, buyer-fund handling, owner payout, loan clearance and transfer timing.
