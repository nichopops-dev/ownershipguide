Confirm the financing basis and upfront cash

For ordinary motor-vehicle loans subject to the MAS framework, the maximum loan-to-value ratio is 70% when the vehicle’s OMV is S$20,000 or less and 60% when it is higher; maximum tenure is seven years. OMV determines the band; it is not the retail car price. Sources: MAS’s financing framework and MOT’s explanation of the limits.

For a hypothetical S$150,000 purchase with OMV above S$20,000, the 60% limit corresponds to S$90,000 financing and S$60,000 outside the loan, before fees and other upfront costs. The actual offer still depends on the lender, borrower and vehicle. Ask the lender to state the valuation basis and any stricter conditions.

Keep your intended reserve outside the downpayment budget. Use the emergency-fund decision, repair reserves and the car cash-buffer plan.

A lower instalment can cost more overall

Assume a S$90,000 loan at a hypothetical 2.8% annual flat rate, with a S$300 fee paid upfront outside the loan. Flat interest here is principal × annual rate × years. This is an illustration, not an available loan offer.

The seven-year option reduces the monthly payment by about S$428.57 but adds S$5,040 in interest. Its slightly lower effective annual rate does not mean less interest in dollars across the longer term. These calculated annual rates compound the monthly cash-flow rate and account for the upfront fee; confirm the convention used in each quote.

MoneySense explains flat rates and effective interest rates. A flat-rate percentage applies to the original principal, so it is not directly comparable with a reducing-balance percentage. Use the repayment schedule, total charges and the same holding period to compare.

Check the monthly household budget separately

Using the five-year loan above, S$1,710 instalments plus an assumed S$700 monthly running budget require S$2,410 each month. With take-home income of S$6,500, other living costs of S$3,200 and planned savings of S$800, only S$90 remains. A 20% income fall would create a S$1,210 monthly shortfall if those commitments stay unchanged.

That is a cash-flow test: do not add depreciation on top of the full instalment as another cash payment. For the economic comparison, use purchase price minus exit value, interest, fees and running costs. See monthly car costs, salary and affordability and the ownership-cost calculator.

Take one comparison sheet to every lender

  1. State the vehicle price, OMV band, downpayment, financed principal and preferred term.
  2. Record the rate basis, effective rate, fees, number of instalments and final payment.
  3. Obtain an early-settlement illustration for your likely sale date, including rebates and charges.
  4. Price running costs and leave the intended reserve available after collection.
  5. Compare the result with ride-hailing and public transport if the budget is tight.

Start with total ownership cost and use the alternative-transport calculator before treating a smaller monthly instalment as permission to buy.

Frequently asked questions

Is OMV the same as the dealer’s selling price?

No. OMV determines the financing-limit band; the vehicle’s retail price is a different amount. Ask the lender to confirm the financing basis.

Does a lower monthly instalment mean a cheaper loan?

No. A longer term or deferred final payment can reduce monthly outflow while increasing total cost or the amount due later.

Can I compare a flat rate directly with an effective rate?

No. Compare rates on the same basis, with fees and repayment timing included, and check the total dollars paid over the intended term.

How should I check an early sale?

Ask for a dated settlement quotation and realistic net sale proceeds. Compare them before relying on an advertised monthly payment or assumed interest rebate.
