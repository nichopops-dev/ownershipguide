A transport diary makes the comparison useful

Record a normal week and an awkward week: work, school, errands, medical appointments, late trips and trips with children or parents. Note passenger needs, luggage, peak-time availability and how often a second vehicle would actually be used.

For a hypothetical month, S$160 of public transport + S$420 of ride-hailing + S$180 of occasional rental totals S$760. If a complete ownership model estimates S$2,600 monthly over the same horizon, the difference is S$1,840 a month. These are assumed budgets, not current fares or typical spending.

Decide what that difference buys: time saved, accessibility or reliable availability on specific trips. Recheck the comparison if the alternative cannot handle a recurring journey; a cheaper budget that leaves the household stranded is incomplete.

Before you commit

  1. Use actual vehicle and insurance quotes, and check what each price includes.
  2. Test a lower resale value, a significant repair and a temporary income reduction.
  3. Check the settlement amount if you sell before the loan ends.
  4. Retain a workable fallback for servicing, breakdowns and days the usual transport is unavailable.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I start if I am unsure about owning a car?

Record the trips your household needs, compare workable alternatives, then run the car-versus-ride-hailing and affordability calculators.

Is the monthly loan payment the cost of owning the car?

It is one cash-flow item. Add running costs for affordability, and separately compare purchase price less exit value, interest, fees and running expenses over your holding period.

Where are the motorcycle guides?

Use the motorcycle starting route for ownership cost, car comparison and COE renewal. The expanded directory includes the existing inspection, insurance, financing and selling guides.
