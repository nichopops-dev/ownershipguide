Used EV Singapore: Pass the Battery and Purchase Gates

Fast path

Use this six-gate checklist for a specific car, price and seller. If any gate lacks evidence, pause the deposit and obtain it. A lower price alone does not resolve unknown battery condition or an unworkable charging routine.

Decision rule: Buy when the LTA facts, condition, warranty, charging routine, repair route and purchase-to-exit cost all work together. Reject or reprice the car when a critical fact cannot be verified.

Check battery evidence →Compare EV and petrol cost

A used EV shares the normal used-car risks of condition, ownership and depreciation. It also depends on battery evidence and a repeatable charging plan. The right comparison is this vehicle over your intended holding period against a real alternative, not its asking price against a new EV's showroom price.

Confirm the vehicle and the seller

Follow LTA's transfer guidance: match make, model, chassis/VIN and registered details; check that financing and road tax issues are resolved; arrange insurance and confirm transfer into your name. Verify the actual COE expiry and any PARF/COE rebate through LTA rather than a listing's estimate. Rebates depend on the vehicle's history and applicable rules, including net ARF where relevant.

Identify whether the seller is a business, a direct owner or acting on consignment. CASE says the Lemon Law does not cover consumer-to-consumer sales; LTA also warns buyers about direct-owner and consignment transactions. Do not assume a dealer's protection applies to a private transaction. Put condition, accessories, battery claims, rectification promises and deposit/refund terms in the written sale agreement.

Make the battery claim testable

Ask for a dated VIN-linked state-of-health or usable-capacity assessment, its method and fault-code result. Then use your route and charge window to decide whether the available energy is enough. A dashboard percentage or predicted range alone is weak evidence. Check service and collision records and obtain an independent or manufacturer-approved inspection as appropriate. Confirm remaining warranty in both time and kilometres.

Prove the routine, not the map pin

Price electricity as delivered and billed kWh, including any parking or idle charges, and test availability at the times you will actually charge. If you rely on public charging, run the four-week practical test. A charger near home is not sufficient if it regularly adds a detour or wait. Include the used battery's measured route range, not its original brochure figure.

Pause when the evidence is missing

  • VIN or LTA details do not match the listing, or outstanding finance/transfer issues are unresolved.
  • The seller will not permit independent inspection or cannot explain an active high-voltage or charging warning.
  • Battery health or warranty is promised verbally but not documented for this car.
  • The route works only at advertised range, or public charging has no reliable backup.
  • The price case depends on a resale figure with no credible basis, or the car's COE ends before your intended use.

Write the go/no-go card

  1. Vehicle: VIN, COE expiry, confirmed rebate and seller type.
  2. Condition: inspection result, battery assessment and unresolved faults.
  3. Protection: written warranty end date/km and repair contact.
  4. Use: tested route buffer and charging site, time and cost.
  5. Money: total 36-month cost versus a specific alternative, plus a downside exit and repair scenario.

Proceed only when the five lines are supported. If the car is sound but priced above its risk-adjusted value, negotiate from the evidence. If a safety or identity question remains unresolved, walk away.

FAQ

What do I check first?

Match the car to LTA records and establish that transfer can proceed, then inspect vehicle and battery condition.

Is a health screenshot enough?

No. Seek a dated VIN-linked assessment with method and fault status.

Does Lemon Law cover a private sale?

CASE excludes consumer-to-consumer sales, and LTA warns about direct-owner and consignment transactions; verify the legal seller.

Is the lowest price the best value?

No. Compare purchase-to-exit loss and running costs over the same period.
