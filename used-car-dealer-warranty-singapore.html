Used-Car Dealer Warranty: Separate the Contract From Statutory Rights

A dealer warranty is a contract with stated coverage and exclusions. Lemon Law rights arise under consumer law. One does not replace the other. Read the seller identity, warranty schedule and sale agreement together before assigning any value to “warranty included”.

Two layers, two questions

For the contractual warranty, ask what the dealer promises and how a claim is authorised. For the Lemon Law, ask whether this is a business-to-consumer sale of goods that did not conform to the contract. Direct-owner and business-to-business transactions are outside the coverage described by CASE.

What the Lemon Law does and does not mean

CASE states that second-hand goods and motorcars are included, while satisfactory quality takes account of age and price. Remedies may include repair, replacement, price reduction or rescission where goods do not conform to contract. Consumer-to-consumer transactions are excluded. Known defects, consumer-caused damage or misuse, attempted repair damage, change of mind and wear and tear are also outside the stated remedies. CASE CPFTA and Lemon Law overview.

The CASE SAFE guide explains that a defect discovered within six months is presumed to have existed at delivery, subject to rebuttal and questions such as wear. It also says the dealer should first have an opportunity to repair a qualifying defect within a reasonable time without significant inconvenience before other remedies are pursued. Compare the warranty with the independent report. A drivetrain-only warranty does not reduce uncertainty about an air-conditioning, electronics or suspension finding unless the written definitions include it.

Worked example: claim limit is not economic value

Assume a comparable dealer car with the warranty costs S$2,500 more. A later repair bill is S$4,800. The contract accepts S$3,600 of parts and labour, applies a S$600 excess, and pays S$3,000. The owner pays the remaining S$1,800, including S$800 of excluded diagnosis, towing or transport.

The result is S$500 ahead only in this stated claim scenario. With no qualifying claim, the premium remains S$2,500. With an excluded wear item, the contractual payment could be zero. Do not treat the maximum claim cap as cash value or assume a failure probability without evidence.

A warranty may still have non-financial value if the process is clear and the household cannot tolerate a large early repair. Price that benefit consciously and keep an accessible buffer for exclusions and downtime.

If a fault appears after delivery

  1. Stop using the vehicle if continued driving could worsen damage or affect safety.
  2. Record the symptoms, date, mileage, warning lights and communications.
  3. Notify the dealer or warranty provider before authorising outside repair where the contract requires approval.
  4. Ask for the diagnosis, coverage decision, buyer share and repair timeline in writing.
  5. Keep replaced-part details, invoices and collection records.
  6. If contractual and statutory positions differ, keep them separate when requesting a response or seeking CASE assistance.

Do not let a warranty promise replace the pre-purchase independent evaluation. Use the records checklist to preserve evidence and the route guide to verify who is selling the vehicle.

Decision rule

Pay a warranty premium only when the covered risks match the inspected car, the exclusions and claims process are workable, and the price remains acceptable without assuming the maximum payout. Treat Lemon Law rights as a separate legal layer and confirm that the transaction falls within its consumer-to-business scope.

Frequently asked questions

Is a dealer warranty the same as Lemon Law protection?

No. The warranty is a contract with its own scope and claim process. Lemon Law rights are statutory and depend on the transaction and facts.

Does the Lemon Law cover every used-car fault?

No. CASE lists limits including disclosed faults, consumer-caused damage or misuse, attempted repair damage and wear and tear. Age and price also affect satisfactory-quality assessment.

Is the warranty claim cap its value?

No. Actual value depends on whether a qualifying failure occurs, which costs are covered, the excess or caps, and the premium paid for the warranty package.
