Report First, Then Compare the Routes

A private settlement does not mean keeping the accident from your insurer. Singapore’s Motor Claims Framework requires each party to report the accident to their insurer even when both parties intend to settle privately.

Reporting and claiming are separate decisions. Complete the first-24-hour accident checklist, then decide whether a written private agreement can close the matter or whether insurer-led handling is safer.

Route rule

Use a private settlement only when the injury, damage, liability and counterparty risks are small enough to define in writing. Convenience at the roadside is not evidence that the file is safe to close.

A foreign vehicle, hit-and-run, pedestrian or cyclist, damage to government property, fatality, or specified injury also engages the Traffic Police reporting rules. Do not use a private agreement to bypass a required report.

Build a closed private-settlement file

Use your insurer’s current form and follow its submission instructions. A complete file should contain:

  • the accident date, time, location and vehicle numbers;
  • the parties’ names, identification details, addresses, contacts and signatures;
  • scene, vehicle and damage photographs plus preserved dashcam footage;
  • the repair quotation or other written basis for the agreed amount;
  • whether neither party pays or one party pays a specified sum;
  • an acknowledgement when money has been received;
  • the settlement wording and the date each party signed; and
  • proof that the accident and completed form were submitted to the insurer.

AIG’s current mutual settlement form, for example, records the accident, vehicles, parties, agreed treatment, compensation and receipt. It is an example of the documentation structure. Use the form required by your own insurer.

Do not negotiate from the bumper photograph

Consider a hypothetical low-speed impact. The first visual estimate is S$650. A workshop later quotes S$720 after inspecting the clips and paint. The parties agree on S$720, document the repair basis, acknowledge payment and submit the insurer’s form.

Insurer report and form submissionStill required

If inspection instead reveals S$1,600 of damage, the route should be reconsidered before anyone signs or pays. The point of the inspection is to prevent an informal guess from becoming a disputed promise.

This example is hypothetical. It does not determine liability or what an insurer will accept. Do not admit liability or agree wording you do not understand; follow the insurer’s instructions.

When the insurer route is doing useful work

Insurer-led handling becomes more valuable as uncertainty increases. It creates a formal evidence and repair process, applies the contract’s liability and coverage terms, and protects against treating an incomplete damage estimate as final.

For third-party property-damage claims, repair timing can matter because the other insurer may need an opportunity to inspect the vehicle before repairs begin. GIA describes a pre-repair survey process intended to align the parties on damage and repair scope. Ask the insurer or appointed workshop when repairs may start.

If you are comparing an own-policy claim with self-funding your own repair, use the claim cash test. It separates the applicable excess, uncovered cash and possible NCD path from the repair invoice.

Before signing or paying

  • Both insurer reports are complete or arranged within the deadline.
  • No injury or reportable condition has been ignored.
  • The parties, vehicles, facts and damage are documented.
  • The settlement uses the insurer’s current form.
  • The amount and payment timing are precise.
  • The recipient will acknowledge payment.
  • Both parties retain the signed form and supporting file.

FAQ

Do I still report an accident if we settle privately?

Yes. The Motor Claims Framework requires the parties to report to their respective insurers even when they agree on a private settlement. A private agreement does not replace the insurer report.

When should I avoid a private settlement?

Avoid rushing into it when anyone is injured, a police-report category applies, liability or damage is unclear, more than two parties are involved, the other party is uncooperative, or the agreement cannot close the matter clearly.

What should a private settlement file contain?

Use your insurer’s form and keep the accident details, parties and vehicles, photographs, repair basis, agreed amount, payment acknowledgement, settlement wording, signatures, dates, and proof that the form was submitted to the insurer.

Does reporting an accident automatically mean I am making a claim?

No. Reporting records the accident and is required under the framework. Whether you or another party proceeds with a claim is a separate matter governed by the facts and policy.
