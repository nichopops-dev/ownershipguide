Shared Car or Ride-Hailing: Price the Trip You Actually Make

Decision rule: Quote the whole outing, not one leg or one hour. Ride-hailing charges for each booked transfer; a shared car charges for the booking window and distance while you keep it, plus applicable fees. Compare access, driving and return obligations separately from cash.

LTA says short-period shared-car charges vary by vehicle, time and distance, with return usually to an original or designated location. PTC says ride-hail flat fares are dynamically set and shown at booking. Use quotes for your actual dates and routes; the examples here are invented to demonstrate the method.

First match the same outing

Define start, stops, destination, passengers, luggage and when the journey ends. For rides, add every outward, inter-stop and return fare. For sharing, include driving and parked time until return, distance, booking/platform charge, optional cover, parking/ERP and any pickup or drop-off travel. If a provider includes fuel or charging, do not add it again. A shared car makes you the driver; a ride does not.

Direct return: two rides can cost less

Imagine a nearby activity with two S$28 ride quotes, outward and home. Total rides cost S$56. A hypothetical shared-car booking for the same outing costs S$50 for the time window, S$9 for distance, S$2 in booking fees and S$5 in destination parking: S$66. The rides cost S$10 less. The example assumes the shared car is within walking distance and fuel is included in the quoted booking. If you need to pay to reach it, sharing costs more.

Multi-stop day: one booking can cost less

Now imagine four separate ride legs at S$28 each: S$112. For the same five-hour day, suppose a shared booking costs S$75 in time, S$20 in distance, S$2 in booking fees and S$8 in parking: S$105. Sharing costs S$7 less on these quotes. An extra hour, parking stop or pickup ride could reverse that small gap; a cancelled ride or unavailable shared car could also change the practical answer.

Copy this worksheet for a real trip

  1. Ride route: save each app fare for the intended time, vehicle size and number of legs. Include any unavoidable return trip.
  2. Shared route: quote the full booking window, including stops and return, and the expected kilometres. Add charges the operator displays outside time and mileage.
  3. Access: record the pickup walk or fare, return location, vehicle size, driving eligibility and a substitute if booking fails.
  4. Compare: subtract the smaller complete cash total from the larger. If the difference is narrow, test a later return and peak ride fares separately.

One operator's help page describes time, mileage and platform charges, with optional damage-waiver cost. That is an example of how to inspect a quote, not a rate to copy across operators.

Check the contract as well as the price

Review the chosen provider's permitted drivers, return point, extension/late fees, fuel or charging rule and accident excess. Excess is potential downside exposure, not a charge that occurs on every normal trip. Operator terms show why the booking screen and contract matter. For ride-hailing, check whether a suitable vehicle is available when the trip must begin, especially with children, mobility needs or bulky items.

Repeat the comparison across your household

Track ten representative outings rather than choosing a permanent winner from one trip. A household may use public transport for commutes, rides for direct late trips, and a shared car for a planned multi-stop day. Count simultaneous needs: one booked shared car cannot handle two separate journeys, while two rides may. If these mixed modes are replacing an owned car, total them over a month in the car-sharing versus ownership guide.

Choose by complete cost and trip fit

Use the lower cash route only if it also gets everyone where they need to be. A car booked for hours you do not drive can be dearer than two short rides. Several separate rides can be dearer than one booking, but not always. For recurring exact-time trips, test availability at the actual time rather than inferring it from an off-peak quote.

FAQ

Which is cheaper?

Price the same outing with complete current quotes. Direct and multi-stop patterns can produce different answers.

Does parked time count?

It generally remains within the shared-car booking window while you keep the vehicle. Check the operator's exact rule.

What if no shared car is available?

Price a realistic backup ride or rental and treat any unserved critical trip as a separate access problem.
