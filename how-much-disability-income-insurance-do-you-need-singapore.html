How Much Disability Income Cover Do You Need in Singapore?

In this selected household, S$4,800 essentials less S$1,500 continuing partner support gives S$3,300 monthly need. A S$3,000 benefit leaves S$300 to fund each paying month, as well as the earlier claim wait.

Check the benefit you can obtain and keep receiving

The S$3,300 expense gap is a planning need, not a right to buy that amount of cover. Insurers apply eligibility, occupational and financial underwriting and policy terms. Confirm the insured amount and what is payable after any income-related adjustments, other benefits, recovery or occupation tests. Do not treat a headline replacement percentage as a universal rule.

MoneySense distinguishes disability-income support from medical bills and care benefits for severe disability. The claim depends on the policy's inability-to-work definition and continuous qualification through its deferred period. Losing a job or being unable to perform one journey does not automatically qualify. Check self-employed duties and earnings evidence where relevant.

Assign the wait, monthly gap and separate bills once

Starting cash is assigned to an S$18,000 income-gap reserve, S$8,000 medical allowance and S$2,400 dated annual-bill fund: S$28,400 after the selected new premium is paid. The annual-bill fund covers known non-monthly bills outside essentials. The medical allowance covers selected additional costs after other medical cover, not the same household payments again.

Twelve months of S$3,300 gap is S$39,600. Nine S$3,000 receipts total S$27,000, leaving S$12,600 net draw and S$5,400 in the income reserve. Peak draw before a benefit arrives is S$15,600, leaving a lowest income-reserve balance S$2,400. Three months of deferral alone costs S$9,900; cash paid before the first month-four receipt is S$13,200.

Test support loss, partial recovery and claim failure

If partner support also stops for the entire year, the monthly gap becomes S$4,800. With the same nine S$3,000 benefits, net draw is S$30,600, S$12,600 beyond the S$18,000 income reserve. Peak pre-receipt draw is S$33,600. A benefit sized around dependable partner support needs revisiting if that support is exposed to the same shock.

For a separate partial-recovery case, keep partner support and assume main-earner take-home pay resumes at S$1,500 in months seven through twelve. The contract is assumed to adjust the payable benefit to S$1,200 in those months; this is a selected term, not a universal reduction formula. Each recovered month still needs S$600 from cash: S$4,800 − S$1,500 partner − S$1,500 earnings − S$1,200 benefit.

First-three-month draw S$9,900 plus months four–six S$900 plus months seven–twelve S$3,600 totals S$14,400, leaving S$3,600 from the S$18,000 income reserve. Benefits total S$16,200 and recovered earnings S$9,000. Different contracts may preserve or adjust benefits differently, so a simple salary subtraction is not enough.

If no benefit qualifies, the year needs S$39,600, S$21,600 beyond the S$18,000 income pool. The same pre-payment need arises if all selected benefits are delayed until year-end; assumed retrospective receipts do not remove earlier deadlines. Test these cases without silently using medical or bill money twice.

Match payment duration to the period of dependence

Check maximum payments for one claim, total limits, recovery tests, recurrence rules and expiry age separately. A policy that stays in force to age 65 can still have a shorter maximum benefit period. S$3,000 for sixty months is S$180,000 nominal payments only if those payments qualify; it is not a lump sum available today or lifetime protection.

In the normal budget, S$5,400 main-earner take-home pay plus S$1,500 partner support, less S$4,800 essentials and a separate S$600 goal, leaves S$1,500 before new cover. S$720 annual provision consumes S$60 monthly and leaves S$1,440. With that goal unchanged, a S$1,500 fall in main-earner take-home pay would erase the pre-policy surplus and leave a S$60 monthly shortfall. Pausing a goal changes that normal-budget stress; unavoidable bills still remain. Review affordable cover and cash needs together rather than presuming future saving will repair every gap.

FAQ

Should I insure a fixed percentage of salary?

Start with essential household payments and dependable support, then compare the gap with benefits available under actual underwriting and policy terms. A percentage alone does not establish the cash needed.

Can I reduce the required cash by an expected benefit?

Model the dated payments and receipts separately. A qualifying benefit can lower eventual reserve draw while leaving earlier deadlines and a monthly residual gap to fund.

Does partial recovery always reduce the benefit by my new earnings?

Use the actual contract. The selected S$1,200 partial-recovery benefit is an assumption; different policies can apply different adjustments, definitions and recovery conditions.

Can the medical and annual-bill pools also count as my full emergency reserve?

Assign each dollar once. After spending from one pool, its balance falls. The combined example leaves S$9,100 across all pools, including only S$5,400 in the income reserve.
