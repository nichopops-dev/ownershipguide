Early vs Severe Critical Illness Insurance in Singapore

Early-stage critical illness cover can pay for a condition that meets a stated early-stage definition. Before paying extra for it, ask how much the early claim pays, what later cover remains and how much cash you need before payment. An early payout can advance part of a shared benefit or be payable separately, depending on the policy.

Read the covered definitions and dates

LIA's framework standardises severe-stage definitions for 37 conditions. Earlier and intermediate stages are defined by insurers under that framework. A medical diagnosis or a label like “early cancer” does not by itself establish a claim: read the exact definition, severity criteria, exclusions and documents required. Ask what happens during a policy waiting period and whether a survival condition applies. Both affect eligibility, separately from the time the insurer takes to pay.

Allow for a gap even if the early claim qualifies

Start a separate two-month illustration with S$8,000 available before the annual premium, which is paid just before the event. S$6,000 of immediate bills and two monthly gaps of S$1,500 require S$9,000 by the end of month two. No other income, cover or funding is assumed beyond what is already reflected in those gaps. Any qualifying early benefit arrives after those payments.

The severe-only option is S$1,600 short for those payments and receives nothing for the selected early event. The shared option is S$1,900 short before receipt, and the additional option is S$2,050 short. With each S$30,000 assumed receipt, their modelled end cash becomes S$28,100 and S$27,950 respectively. Those positive balances assume earlier payments can be funded; they do not erase the shortfalls. A delayed or declined claim means relying on money you can actually access.

Check what a repeat claim would require

Ask for a written benefit ledger: amount now, remaining severe benefit, any reduction in death cover, waiting interval between specified claims, same-condition versus recurrence rules, per-claim limits, aggregate limits and premiums after payment. Do not multiply a headline sum assured by the maximum number of claims without checking which sequences qualify. Keep current cover until you understand replacement acceptance, exclusions and commencement.

Use the recovery cash worksheet to estimate the timing gap, and the premium worksheet to compare sustainable quotes. If the real problem is prolonged loss of income rather than a named diagnosis, compare disability income with CI.

FAQ

Does early CI automatically add to my severe-stage cover?

No. It can advance part of a shared benefit or be payable separately. The chosen shared example leaves S$70,000 after S$30,000 is paid from S$100,000.

Does every early diagnosis qualify?

No. Check the contract’s exact condition and stage, any waiting or survival requirement, and its exclusions. Early stages are not standardised by the LIA severe-stage framework.

Can I assume a later recurrence is covered?

No. Verify the event definition, required intervals, same-condition rules and remaining per-claim and aggregate limits.

Can an accepted early claim replace the cash I need before payment?

No. Work out when bills are due and when the benefit is received. In the selected shared example, the household is S$1,900 short before the assumed payout.
