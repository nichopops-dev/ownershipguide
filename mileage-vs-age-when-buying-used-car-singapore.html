Read Mileage, Age and Records as One Timeline

Age measures elapsed time. Mileage records use. Neither proves condition. Start with the two numbers as a screening ratio, then replace the ratio with dated records and an independent evaluation. A very low odometer without a traceable timeline is an unanswered question, not a premium by itself.

The practical rule

Calculate, reconcile, inspect, price. Calculate rough annualised mileage. Reconcile the actual readings and service dates. Inspect the present condition. Then decide whether the price recognises the remaining uncertainty and the car’s registration horizon.

Use annualised mileage as a question generator

For a rough first pass:

Annualised mileage = current odometer ÷ age in years.

The result is an average across the car’s entire age. It does not show whether use was steady, whether the odometer is accurate or how the car was driven. Use it to locate periods that need evidence.

Car B has the lower number, but its missing timeline prevents a clean comparison. If later records support the mileage and an evaluation supports condition, it may deserve a premium. Until then, Car A has the stronger evidence file.

Do not invent a universal “normal kilometres per year” threshold. Your useful benchmark is the pattern across the exact cars you are comparing, supported by dates and documents.

Reconcile the mileage timeline

  1. List every dated odometer reading from service invoices, inspection reports and seller records.
  2. Sort them by date and check that readings move forward consistently.
  3. Mark long gaps, sudden changes and periods with no supporting documents.
  4. Compare the service work with time as well as kilometres; ask how the stated maintenance schedule was followed.
  5. Carry the unresolved items into the independent evaluation brief.

A gap does not prove misconduct. It reduces what the records can establish. Price and decision confidence should reflect that limitation instead of replacing it with a guess.

Use condition to test the story

Ask the evaluator to compare the displayed mileage and stated use with observable condition, including controls, seats, tyres, underbody, fluids, fault codes and model-specific wear points. The purpose is not to “certify” the odometer from appearance. It is to identify inconsistencies that need an explanation.

Keep records review separate from inspection. A clean service file does not prove present condition, and a good test drive does not fill a missing history. Use the records checklist and inspection sequence as two different evidence streams.

Age also changes the ownership horizon

Registration date affects more than the age label. It determines remaining COE and can affect PARF eligibility and the applicable rebate schedule. LTA states that eligible cars must be no more than 10 years old when deregistered to receive a PARF rebate, and that the schedule depends on registration and COE timing. Changes apply to cars registered with COEs from the second bidding exercise in February 2026 onward. Verify the actual vehicle and intended deregistration date instead of copying a generic table into your valuation. LTA PARF and COE rebate guidance.

LTA’s transfer guidance tells buyers to obtain an access code from the owner and verify age, COE expiry and PARF eligibility through the transfer-fee enquiry. These facts define the time available for ownership and the exit assumptions in your cost model. LTA transfer guidance.

Price the car after the evidence review

Compare at least four items together: purchase price, known immediate work, expected running costs over the same holding period and gross exit value. Keep an unused repair reserve separate as a liquidity requirement. The used-versus-new calculator can compare the lifecycle cash flows once you have a written quote and a settlement estimate.

A lower-mileage car can justify a higher price when the timeline, condition and ownership horizon support it. A higher-mileage car can be the stronger purchase when the records are continuous, the inspection is satisfactory and the price recognises the use.

Questions to ask before the viewing

  • Which dated documents support the current odometer reading?
  • Are there gaps in service or inspection records, and why?
  • What use pattern does the seller describe during each ownership period?
  • Which wear items have been replaced, and where are the invoices?
  • What are the verified registration date, COE expiry and PARF eligibility?
  • Will the seller permit an independent evaluation that addresses the gaps?

Decision rule

Prefer the car whose age, mileage, dated records, present condition and price tell the most coherent story. Treat annualised mileage as a screen. Treat official vehicle details, documents and independent findings as the evidence used to decide.

Frequently asked questions

Is lower mileage always better on a used car?

No. Lower mileage is useful only when dated records, physical condition and the seller’s account support it. Mileage cannot replace an independent evaluation.

How do I compare mileage on cars of different ages?

Divide the current odometer reading by the car’s age for a rough annualised screen, then reconcile the actual timeline from dated service and inspection records.

Why does registration date matter beyond vehicle age?

It determines remaining COE and can affect PARF eligibility and the applicable rebate schedule. Verify the actual vehicle through LTA services rather than estimating from a listing.
