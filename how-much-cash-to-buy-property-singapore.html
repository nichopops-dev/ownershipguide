How Much Cash to Buy Property in Singapore? (2026)

Calculate two figures before committing to a purchase: the money ultimately funded from cash, and the larger cash balance you may need before CPF reimbursement or sale proceeds arrive. A cost being CPF-eligible does not guarantee CPF can pay it on the day it falls due.

Start with the actual loan offer

For a qualifying first bank housing loan at 75% LTV, the usual minimum cash portion is 5% of the lower of price and valuation. The remaining downpayment can use eligible CPF or cash. At a reduced 55% LTV, the minimum cash portion is 10%; with existing housing loans, the cash requirement can be 25%. Use the full LTV table for your route. HDB loans have a 75% ceiling and no mandatory cash downpayment if eligible CPF can cover it. Any price above valuation must be paid in cash. For a completed property, the CPF charge is generally lodged at legal completion, so stamp duty paid earlier normally needs cash bridging before reimbursement. Certain uncompleted-property transactions can use CPF directly if the charge is lodged within the duty deadline. HDB buyers should confirm the process with HDB. Ask the lawyer for a dated funding schedule. CPF Board: eligible property fees and reimbursement timing.

Worked example: S$800,000 resale flat with an HDB loan

Assume an eligible buyer gets the full 75% loan, price equals valuation, no ABSD applies, and sufficient OA is available. Loan: S$600,000. Downpayment: S$200,000. BSD: S$1,800 + S$3,600 + S$13,200 = S$18,600. Downpayment plus BSD totals S$218,600.

This is a funding requirement, not automatically S$218,600 cash. Eligible CPF may cover those items, subject to timing. Add a hypothetical S$3,000 for fees, S$40,000 renovation and moving budget, and S$30,000 retained cash reserve: total resources required become S$291,600. Those three additions are planning assumptions, not fee quotations. If OA pays S$218,600 and the S$3,000 fees are paid in cash, the ultimate cash allocation is S$73,000; any earlier stamp-duty payment temporarily increases the cash needed.

Worked example: S$1.5 million condo with a first bank loan

Assume price equals valuation, 75% LTV, no ABSD and enough eligible OA. Loan: S$1,125,000. Downpayment: S$375,000, including S$75,000 mandatory cash and S$300,000 cash or CPF. BSD: S$44,600. Total downpayment plus BSD: S$419,600. IRAS: stamp-duty rates.

If CPF eventually funds S$300,000 of downpayment and all BSD, S$75,000 remains the ultimate cash portion of those two items. Before BSD reimbursement, cash needed for them can reach S$119,600. Add legal costs, valuation, renovation, moving, reserves and any buyer-agent fee actually agreed. Option and exercise payments are normally part of the purchase price; do not add them again to the full downpayment.

What changes the answer?

A lower valuation creates both a cash-only premium and a smaller loan base. ABSD can add a large separate duty; confirm citizenship, ownership count, joint-buyer treatment and any remission with IRAS. A smaller income-approved loan increases the amount funded outside the loan. CPF withdrawal limits, age and remaining lease may restrict the OA you can use.

For an upgrade, put sale completion, outstanding-loan settlement, CPF refund and new-purchase payment dates on one timeline. A refund expected next month does not pay this week’s duty. FAQ

Can CPF pay stamp duty and legal fees?

Eligible CPF OA savings can pay BSD, ABSD and eligible legal fees. Stamp duty generally needs cash first and later reimbursement; confirm the transaction-specific timing with HDB or your lawyer.

Does every purchase need 5% cash?

No. The cash requirement depends on the loan route, LTV and existing loans. An HDB loan has no mandatory cash downpayment if eligible CPF is sufficient, but valuation premiums and other cash costs can still apply.

Is the minimum cash downpayment the full cash budget?

No. Include duty timing, CPF limits, fees, renovation, moving and the cash reserve you intend to retain.
