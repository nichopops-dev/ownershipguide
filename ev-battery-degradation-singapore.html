EV Battery Degradation Singapore: Check Range and Evidence

Fast path

Use this page to test the battery condition of a particular EV. For coverage, go to battery warranty; for a diagnosed fault, go to repair exposure.

Decision rule: Get a dated, vehicle-specific battery assessment, convert its usable-energy result into your real route and charging window, then check whether the remaining buffer is adequate through your holding period. A dashboard range estimate alone does not answer the question.

Battery degradation is a gradual reduction in usable capacity or performance. It is different from a component fault or a pack that cannot operate. A used EV can have some loss and still suit your trips; conversely, a small loss can matter if the car barely covers a repeated journey without an inconvenient charging stop. Evaluate the particular vehicle and route, not an internet average.

Three pieces of evidence to request

  1. Battery assessment: a report tied to the vehicle identification number (VIN), date and odometer, showing the method, reported state of health (SoH) or usable capacity, and any fault codes. Ask who produced it and whether the manufacturer recognises the method for warranty claims.
  2. Vehicle history: service and collision records, battery or drive-unit work, software or recall work, and any unexplained charging or power-limitation warnings.
  3. Representative drive: observe consumption, charging behaviour and alerts on a route resembling your own. Record weather, load and state-of-charge (SoC) start/end. This is a practical fit check, not a precise laboratory SoH test.

A dashboard's “100% charge” means a full charge of the capacity currently available to the car. Its predicted kilometres respond to consumption assumptions. Neither number, by itself, establishes the battery's original-versus-current usable capacity. Different tools can also define SoH differently. Ask for the underlying measurement and its uncertainty rather than accepting a single percentage without context.

What the result means

Separate capacity loss, displayed range and failure. Capacity loss reduces energy available between charge limits; displayed range is an estimate; failure needs a diagnosis. Warranty capacity thresholds, where offered, are contractual claim triggers rather than a promise that every car above the threshold has comfortable range. Check the actual warranty document for this VIN.

Why two similar cars can differ

Battery age, climate, driving and charging patterns, cell chemistry and thermal design all affect useful life, as the US Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center explains. The previous owner's fast-charging share is only one clue; do not infer SoH from it alone. A good inspection combines documentation with the vehicle's current behaviour.

Convert usable capacity into your range buffer

Here is a hypothetical calculation, not a claim about any Singapore model. Suppose the original usable capacity was 60 kWh, a credible current assessment says 54 kWh, your planned charge window is 10%–80%, and the car uses 18 kWh per 100 km on your route. Current window energy is 54 × 70% = 37.8 kWh, or 210 km. The original window would have been 42 kWh, or about 233 km. The difference is about 23 km per such window.

Use vehicle consumption in kWh/100 km for this range calculation. Electricity delivered and billed at the charger can be higher because of charging losses; use the delivered figure for cost, not for driving range. Add a buffer for traffic, detours, rain, passengers and charging availability. If your repeated 190 km trip would leave only 20 km before the chosen lower limit, test that trip before buying. If your repeated trip is 45 km, the same capacity result may be immaterial.

Singapore ownership context

Check range against your actual parking and charging routine. A car that covers the daily trip can still be a poor fit if the needed charger is often unavailable or charging takes a special detour. Use the four-week charging test if you lack dependable home charging. Also compare the car's remaining COE life with your planned holding period; an impressive range figure cannot fix a mismatched vehicle horizon.

If a seller refuses reasonable battery and vehicle inspection, the missing evidence is itself a purchasing fact. A reduced price may compensate for a measurable shortfall; it cannot tell you whether an undiagnosed warning is benign.

Accept, reprice or walk away

  1. Accept: evidence is traceable, there are no unresolved faults, your tested range has a comfortable buffer, and the whole used-EV case works.
  2. Reprice: condition is measurable but weaker than assumed. Recalculate route convenience and future resale using the actual result, then seek a price that makes sense for that car.
  3. Pause or walk: the report is not verifiable, the seller blocks inspection, the route only works on ideal conditions, or there is an unresolved high-voltage warning.

FAQ

Does a lower dashboard range prove degradation?

No. It is an estimate. Ask for documented battery testing and check real consumption.

Is 90% health unusable?

No. Apply the capacity to your charge window and route. The resulting buffer is what matters.

What if there is no report?

Request one or price the condition as unknown; do not substitute the seller's screenshot for evidence.

Is degradation the same as failure?

No. A fault needs diagnosis and may raise a different warranty question.
