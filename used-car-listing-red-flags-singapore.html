Filter the Listing Before You Book a Viewing

A listing earns a viewing when it identifies the car and seller, makes the price comparable and gives you a practical route to independent proof. Photos and adjectives can start the conversation. They cannot verify registration details, condition, service history or the finance package.

Use three outcomes

Shortlist when the core facts are present and verifiable. Clarify when a specific missing item could change the ranking. Pass when the seller blocks verification, changes the story or demands commitment before basic checks.

A blank is a question, not automatically a defect. The response matters: a clear answer with a document improves the file; a shifting answer or refusal changes the risk.

Move from listing claims to official and independent proof

LTA advises a second-hand vehicle buyer to verify registration details such as age, COE expiry and PARF eligibility through the transfer-fee enquiry using an access code from the current owner. It also advises buyers to tally the make, model, engine and chassis identifiers with LTA records and the physical vehicle. LTA transfer and used-vehicle buyer guidance.

  1. Record the listing as published: URL, date, stated mileage, price and promises.
  2. Ask who owns the car and who will be named as seller in the agreement.
  3. Request the registration facts and access needed to verify them before commitment.
  4. Ask for dated service evidence and invoices, not a summary such as “fully maintained”.
  5. Confirm that an independent evaluation is allowed and who pays if it reveals a material mismatch.
  6. Ask for an itemised written quote before comparing affordability.

Use the records checklist to reconcile dates and mileage. Use the inspection checklist for the physical car. A listing should point to those checks, not replace them.

Red flags that change the order of work

The seller relies on one flattering number

Low mileage, few owners or a low monthly instalment may be relevant. Each still needs context. Annualise the mileage, check the timeline and compare the written total quote. Do not let one headline suppress the rest of the file.

The transaction route is blurred

A dealer listing can still be a consignment sale. LTA states that direct-owner and consignment purchases are not covered by the Lemon Law. Identify the contracting party before assigning value to dealer protection. See the dealer-versus-direct-owner comparison.

Urgency arrives before evidence

Another interested buyer may exist, but it does not verify this car. Treat a time limit as a constraint on the decision; do not treat it as proof. If the available time cannot accommodate essential checks, the practical answer is to pass.

The seller will not state what the price includes

Ask for vehicle price, required fees, financing conditions, warranty, trade-in and optional extras as separate lines. A listing is not comparable when a rebate, loan condition or trade-in assumption is silently embedded.

Turn a passing listing into a viewing brief

Carry a short written brief to the viewing:

  • the facts you expect to match: variant, identifiers, odometer and registration details;
  • the claims that need physical or documentary proof;
  • the known defects already reflected in price;
  • the independent evaluation condition;
  • the itemised price and finance assumptions you will compare later.

The CASE SAFE checklist separates dealer checks from professional evaluation and invites the buyer to compare the two. That is a useful principle even when a listing looks complete: seller disclosure and independent findings should reconcile. CASE SAFE checklist.

Decision rule

View the best-documented cars first. Clarify a listing only when one answer could improve its ranking. Pass when the seller prevents verification, hides the basis of the quote or asks you to accept transaction risk before the car has earned confidence.

For the next step, compare mileage against age and records. Before money changes hands, use the final commitment gate.

Frequently asked questions

Does a weak listing prove that the car is bad?

No. It means the listing has not supplied enough evidence to justify the next step. Ask for the missing facts or rank a better-documented car first.

What should I request before paying for an inspection?

Confirm the seller, registration details, COE expiry, PARF eligibility, service evidence, disclosed defects, itemised price and whether independent evaluation is allowed.

Is a low advertised monthly payment enough to compare listings?

No. Compare the vehicle price, downpayment, loan amount, tenure, fees, total repayment and settlement at your intended exit date.
