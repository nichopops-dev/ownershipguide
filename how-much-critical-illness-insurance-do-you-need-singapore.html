How Much Critical Illness Cover Do You Need in Singapore?

Size critical illness cover from the cost of a selected recovery period. Add the household income gap, uncovered care and one-off costs, then subtract cash genuinely assigned to recovery and benefits that would pay for the same event. Calculate the gap before any claim pays separately: a larger eventual lump sum cannot pay an earlier bill.

Model the cost and duration of recovery

This selected household has S$4,800 essential payments a month, including the full mortgage instalment and existing premiums. A partner contributes S$1,500 each month after their separate commitments. The affected earner receives confirmed S$1,800 monthly net paid leave for the first two months only. Those resources arrive before the related bills. The household gap is S$1,500 in each of those months and S$3,300 thereafter, with discretionary goals paused.

Use cash you can spend and cover that actually matches

Start with S$20,000 accessible savings. Protect S$10,000 for other emergencies and S$3,000 for already committed bills outside this model. That leaves S$7,000 for recovery. Any extra policy premium has to be separately budgeted; if paid from these same savings, reduce the available balance first. Assume an existing S$20,000 CI benefit is in force and qualifies for this selected event.

Find the cash needed before the claim arrives

If the S$20,000 is received at the end of month three, payments before it total S$12,300: S$6,000 up front, S$3,000 in two monthly gaps and S$3,300 in month three. Against S$7,000 assigned cash, the pre-receipt shortfall reaches S$5,300. The sequence is already S$500 short after month one. The example assumes no new benefit arrives before those payments.

If the same benefit is delayed to the end of month six, the household needs S$24,600 first, including the care bill, and is S$17,600 short before the receipt. If it does not qualify at all, the twelve-month gap grows to S$37,400. Do not silently borrow to fill these deficits or treat protected cash as both spent and still available. Plan the actual due dates and confirm any separate source of funding.

Change one assumption at a time

  • Six more months: With the same S$3,300 gap each month and no new one-off costs, total 18-month need is S$64,200; the gap after assigned cash and the qualifying benefit is S$37,200.
  • No paid leave: If the partner contribution continues but paid leave never arrives, add S$3,600; the twelve-month gap after existing funding is S$21,000.
  • Partner support falls: If available support is S$1,000 monthly for all twelve months instead of S$1,500, the twelve-month funding gap rises to S$23,400.

Use rules of thumb as a cross-check

MoneySense lists four times annual income as a broad CI protection rule of thumb. The S$17,400 here is an additional gap for one selected year and one qualifying event after other funding. It is not a competing estimate of total cover for a much longer recovery. Compare several durations and check what would happen to debts, dependants and care if earnings do not recover.

A CI benefit follows a covered definition. If your main concern is an inability to work that may not qualify for CI, use the CI versus disability income comparison and the monthly income-gap worksheet. Then compare matched CI premium schedules and the effect of earlier claims on later cover.

FAQ

Should I simply buy four times my annual income?

MoneySense presents that as a broad rule of thumb. Also estimate several recovery periods, match existing benefits to the event and check cash available before receipt.

Can I subtract every dollar of savings from the lump-sum need?

Only subtract money you can spend on that event after protecting other commitments and reserves. Do not count the same money twice.

Does potential cover solve the shortfall before a claim pays?

No. In the selected month-three case, S$12,300 is due before the receipt and S$7,000 is assigned, leaving a S$5,300 funding shortfall at that point.

Should I count group or existing insurance at its headline value?

Use only the amount that would be payable in the selected scenario. Check its definition, employment or expiry conditions, prior claims, offsets and actual receipt timing.
