Dealer or Direct Owner: Verify the Seller, Price and Process

The route changes who is contracting with you, what help is bundled into the price and which protections may apply. It does not tell you whether the car is good. Compare the written all-in price, independent findings and transaction sequence for the specific vehicle.

Confirm the seller named in the contract

A dealer advertisement, consignment arrangement and direct-owner sale can create different legal positions. LTA states that buyers from a direct owner or consignment dealer are not covered by the Lemon Law. Check who is actually selling the vehicle before assigning value to dealer recourse.

CASE states that the Lemon Law covers second-hand vehicles sold to consumers by businesses, while consumer-to-consumer transactions are excluded. The vehicle’s age, price, known defects and ordinary wear matter when assessing satisfactory quality. CASE Lemon Law overview.

The label used in an advertisement is insufficient. Read the sale agreement, invoice and payment instructions. If a dealer says the car is merely on consignment, clarify the contracting seller and the consequences before paying a deposit.

Worked example: compare all-in price and liquidity

Assume two cars have similar remaining COE and both pass the buyer’s initial suitability screen. The figures are hypothetical and do not establish market prices.

The direct route is S$3,500 cheaper on initial outlay. The repair buffers are not assumed spending and must not be added to ownership cost unless used. They show that a lower purchase price can still require similar cash on hand when formal support is thinner or condition uncertainty is higher.

Replace the example buffers with workshop findings, warranty terms and your own tolerance for downtime. A dealer car with weak records and a narrow warranty may deserve a larger buffer than a transparent direct-owner car.

The same diligence applies to both routes

  1. Use LTA’s transfer-fee enquiry with an access code from the owner to verify registration details, age, COE expiry and PARF eligibility.
  2. Tally the make, model, engine and chassis identifiers with the vehicle and LTA records.
  3. Arrange an independent evaluation and put any deposit, refund and rectification conditions in writing.
  4. Reconcile service records, odometer readings, disclosed defects and physical findings.
  5. Itemise the vehicle price, mandatory fees, financing, warranty, insurance and trade-in separately.

LTA advises buyers to inspect the vehicle physically, verify identifiers, avoid illegal modifications and use the CASE SAFE checklist. LTA guidance for second-hand vehicle buyers. Use the detailed inspection sequence and records reconciliation before commitment.

Make payment, transfer and handover one written sequence

  • Secure financing approval before possession and record every payment milestone.
  • For a dealer, pay the company identified by its UEN and obtain an official company receipt.
  • Keep Singpass credentials private and confirm the transfer yourself.
  • Have valid motor insurance in the buyer’s name before accepting transfer.
  • Check the vehicle details again before confirmation and verify the vehicle appears in the buyer’s OneMotoring dashboard.

LTA requires ownership transfer within seven days when possession changes and states that vehicles being transferred should not have outstanding financing. Decision rule

Choose a dealer when the itemised premium buys useful process support, credible contractual protection and an acceptable inspected car. Choose a direct owner when the documented price advantage compensates for the extra coordination and you can complete the diligence and transfer sequence yourself. Walk away from either route when the seller blocks independent verification or the contract contradicts the sales story.

Frequently asked questions

Is buying from a dealer automatically safer?

No. A dealer route can provide process support and possible business-to-consumer remedies, but the specific car, contract, records, evaluation and payment sequence still need verification.

Does the Lemon Law cover a direct-owner purchase?

CASE states that it does not apply to consumer-to-consumer transactions. LTA also states that direct-owner and consignment purchases are not covered.

Should I subtract a repair buffer from the car’s value?

No. Keep an unused buffer as a liquidity requirement. Record actual or expected repairs separately so cash reserves are not mistaken for spending.
