Critical Illness Insurance Cost in Singapore: Compare Premiums

Compare critical illness (CI) premiums for the same benefit, age at expiry and claim terms, then add what you would actually pay at each renewal. A cheaper first year can still cost more over the period you expect to hold the cover. In the selected 15-year example, the lower starting premium ends up costing S$1,200 more in total.

Match the benefit before comparing price

CI pays a specified cash benefit for a covered illness or surgery that meets the contract definition. It does not reimburse hospital invoices or automatically replace wages every month. Compare the same sum assured, insured stages, contract definition, expiry age, premium-payment period, waiting and survival conditions, exclusions and claim count. For a rider, compare the required base policy too and ask what remains of the death or other benefit after a CI payout.

LIA's 2024 framework standardises definitions for the severe stage of 37 conditions; earlier stages are not standardised by that framework. Its implementation rules phase out sales using the 2019 definitions from 1 October 2025, with transition rules for earlier signed individual proposals and separate rules for group renewals. Check the wording actually issued. A new sale rule does not by itself rewrite an existing individual contract.

After year 11, B is still S$240 cheaper: S$6,360 against A's S$6,600. After year 12, B reaches S$7,320 against A's S$7,200 and becomes S$120 more expensive. The crossover assumes actual payment of the scheduled annual bills and is not a reason to cancel before year 12: later replacement can involve underwriting, new exclusions or loss of continuity.

Separate payment instalments from renewal assumptions

A's S$600 annual cost is S$50 per month to set aside. If the same insurer offered a S$53 monthly payment instead, twelve payments would cost S$636, S$36 more in that year. With B, the annual bills translate to S$35, S$55 and S$80 of monthly provision in the three bands. That does not mean the insurer bills monthly.

If B's annual premium for years 11–15 were 20% above the selected schedule, it would be S$1,152 and total premiums S$11,160, or S$2,160 above A. This is a stress test, not a renewal prediction. Ask which rates are guaranteed, what can be repriced, how renewals work after a claim and whether any promotion expires.

Check cash at the highest scheduled premium

Suppose the household has S$120 monthly surplus before adding CI, with existing protection already budgeted. A leaves S$70 after provision. B leaves S$85 at first, S$65 in the second band and S$40 in the third. The stressed third-band S$96 monthly provision leaves S$24. If the 15-year contract is paid annually, the corresponding cash bill is still due in full on the renewal date. Avoid treating the emergency balance as recurring premium income.

Read the quote and policy together

Record the dated premium table, product summary and policy wording, including different cover and premium terms if applicable. For a bundled life policy, compare the full premium on matched life and CI benefits and keep any surrender value separate. A cheaper quote with a narrower stage definition or a smaller remaining life benefit is a different package.

Use the lump-sum sizing worksheet to choose an amount before pricing. Compare early-stage and severe-stage payment terms, CI versus disability income, and CI versus medical bills for the losses the quote does not cover.

FAQ

Is there one typical CI premium for Singapore?

A quote depends on the applicant, term, benefit, stages, exclusions and payment terms. The figures here are invented schedules for comparison, not a market range.

Is S$50 monthly provision the same as the insurer charging S$50 a month?

No. It is the monthly amount to set aside for S$600 due annually. A separate monthly instalment schedule may have a different total.

Does the lower first-year premium stay cheaper?

Not necessarily. Under the selected schedules, quote B starts cheaper and becomes more expensive on a cumulative basis in year 12. Compare written schedules over the period you expect to hold cover.

Does the LIA severe-stage framework standardise early CI?

No. It standardises severe-stage definitions for 37 conditions; compare the actual early-stage wording, benefit amounts and limits in the contract.
