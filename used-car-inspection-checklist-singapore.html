Used-Car Inspection: Turn Findings Into a Buy, Repair or Walk-Away Decision

A viewing can reject an obviously unsuitable car. It cannot replace an independent professional evaluation. Decide who will inspect the vehicle, what the report covers and what happens to the deposit before money becomes difficult to recover.

Do not confuse three different checks

A buyer walkaround, a pre-purchase professional evaluation and LTA periodic inspection serve different purposes. A periodic inspection checks specified roadworthiness and emissions systems. It is not a complete diagnosis, condition warranty or valuation.

Jump to before the inspection, the evaluation scope, the worked comparison or the decision record.

Set the inspection terms before the deposit

  1. Choose an evaluator independent of the seller and ask what is outside the evaluation scope.
  2. Write down whether a deposit is refundable, transferable or forfeited if material findings arise.
  3. State whether the seller will repair, reduce the price or allow the buyer to withdraw, and who pays for a repeat inspection.
  4. Obtain permission for diagnostic scans, road testing or lifting the vehicle where applicable.
  5. Keep the report, photographs, odometer reading and vehicle identifiers with the sale documents.

The CASE SAFE checklist separates dealer disclosure from independent professional evaluation and says the independent report should take precedence where the two conflict. CASE SAFE checklist for pre-owned cars.

LTA’s periodic inspection covers structural integrity, wheels, braking, steering, body, visual/indication and propulsion systems. LTA periodic-inspection checklist. Use those categories as a roadworthiness baseline, then ask the evaluator what additional diagnostic checks the pre-purchase service includes.

LTA also warns used-vehicle buyers to avoid illegal modifications and to verify engine and chassis markings. A modified vehicle can require rectification and may face regulatory consequences. LTA used-vehicle verification guidance and LTA vehicle-modification rules.

Worked example: the cheaper car can cost more in year one

Assume both vehicles meet the same household need and financing costs are excluded. “Likely within one year” is an evaluator-informed planning estimate, not guaranteed spending.

Hypothetical evaluation findings and repair quotes
First-year purchase caseCar ACar B
Agreed priceS$72,000S$74,000
Independent evaluationS$300S$300
Immediate workS$1,600S$0
Likely work within one yearS$900S$300
Repair downtime or transportS$400S$0
Price plus stated first-year exposureS$75,200S$74,600

Car A is S$2,000 cheaper at purchase but S$600 more expensive after the stated findings. The figures do not prove Car B is better: the report may exclude hidden failures and the likely-work estimate can change. They show why the decision should use price plus findings, not price alone.

If the seller repairs an item, confirm the exact work and obtain the invoice. Do not also subtract the full repair quote from an already reduced price unless both consequences actually apply. A repeat evaluation can be worthwhile when the original finding was material.

Walk-away signals

  • The seller refuses a reasonable independent evaluation or restricts material parts of it.
  • Engine, chassis, registration, mileage or modification information does not reconcile.
  • The deposit terms change after findings appear.
  • A serious fault is dismissed verbally but the seller will not record responsibility in writing.
  • The price only works if every uncertain issue turns out favourably.

Next, reconcile the report with the records checklist. If a dealer warranty is offered, test whether the discovered risks are actually covered with the warranty worksheet.

Frequently asked questions

Is an LTA periodic inspection the same as a pre-purchase evaluation?

No. Periodic inspection checks listed roadworthiness and emissions systems. Ask a pre-purchase evaluator to state its wider diagnostic scope and exclusions.

Should I pay a deposit before inspection?

If a deposit is required, put the evaluation condition, refund or rectification triggers, deadline and responsibility for costs in writing before paying.

What if the dealer checklist and independent report disagree?

The CASE SAFE guidance says the independent professional evaluation should take precedence where it conflicts with the dealer checklist. Resolve the discrepancy in writing before proceeding.
