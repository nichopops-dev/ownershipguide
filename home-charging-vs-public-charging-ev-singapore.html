Treat Charging Access as Part of the Car

The same EV can be easy or exhausting depending on where it charges. Compare the complete charging system: electricity, setup, access rules, deliberate time and what happens when the preferred bay is unavailable.

Comparison rule

Call charging “at home” when it happens reliably during ordinary residential parking. Price it using the actual contract, which may be a household tariff or a shared-charger operator rate.

1) Separate location, tariff and control

ArrangementWhere the car sitsWho controls price and access
Private residential chargerDedicated space at a landed homeOwner chooses equipment; electricity follows the owner's supply plan
Shared residential chargerHDB, condominium or apartment carparkOperator or property arrangement sets tariff, access and idle rules
Workplace or destination chargerWork, retail or another regular stopSite and operator set access hours and price
Separate public-charging tripA location visited mainly to chargeOperator availability and tariff; user absorbs detour and waiting

A residential location does not automatically mean household-rate electricity. Keep the physical routine and payment contract as separate worksheet inputs.

2) Compare five-year cash on the same delivered energy

Assume 2,912 delivered kWh a year, matching an EV driven 16,000 km at 18.2 delivered kWh/100 km. Compare two hypothetical routes:

  • a private home charger with an installed, approved and registered setup cost of S$3,000 and electricity at S$0.3478/kWh; and
  • public AC charging at S$0.55/kWh with no separate access fee.

Public charging is S$55.97 cheaper in this narrow cash example. The home electricity saving almost—but does not quite—recover the setup inside five years. A longer holding period, higher distance, a different home tariff or a different installation quote changes the result.

3) Add deliberate charging time

Suppose the public route creates one deliberate hour a week across detours, waiting and returning to move the car. Over five years that is 260 hours. If the household chooses to value that time at S$15/hour, the planning value is S$3,900.

4) Stress-test availability and fallback

For each charging route, answer:

  • Can the car use the bay during the actual overnight, work or destination parking window?
  • Is booking required, and what happens when the charger is occupied or offline?
  • Does an idle fee force a return before another obligation ends?
  • Which second charger works without risking the next critical trip?
  • Could a move, job change or property decision remove the access?

LTA's MyTransport.SG provides real-time availability for publicly accessible charging points. Observe intended chargers at your normal times for several weeks; a citywide charger count does not answer a site-level availability question.

5) Apply the Singapore property context

MOT reported that all HDB towns were EV-ready and more than 90% of HDB carparks had charging points by December 2025. By March 2026, Singapore had around 30,500 charging points, while 139 HDB carparks—7% of the total—still lacked chargers because of technical constraints or requests to delay installation.

That progress improves the public route but does not guarantee a bay in a particular carpark at a particular time. HDB season-parking holders at affected carparks can check the stated option to transfer to a nearby HDB carpark with chargers at the same season-parking rate.

For a private charger, obtain the complete installation and electrical quotation. LTA requires chargers to be type-approved and registered before use; registration and ongoing responsibility remain part of the setup. Shared chargers serving condominium residents are operated under the EV charging-operator licensing framework.

If home access is unavailable, run the four-week no-home-charging trial. Feed the winning route into the monthly charging budget.

FAQ

Is home EV charging always cheaper than public charging?

No. A lower household electricity rate can be offset by charger installation, registration and maintenance. Compare those setup costs with the public tariff over the same period and distance.

Can public charging be as convenient as home charging?

It can when charging happens during parking you already need. It becomes less convenient when it creates separate trips, queues, return journeys or idle-fee deadlines.

Does a charger in my residential carpark count as home charging?

For routine analysis, yes if access is dependable while the car is normally parked. For cost analysis, use the operator tariff and terms; a shared residential charger may not use the household electricity rate.

What should I verify before installing a private EV charger?

Confirm property and electrical approvals, an LTA type-approved charger, installation and certification requirements, registration, inspection responsibilities, warranty, maintenance and the full quotation.
