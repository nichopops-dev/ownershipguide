TDSR and MSR Singapore (2026): 55%, 30% and Assessment Rates

TDSR and MSR compare assessed repayments with recognised gross monthly income. The usual limits are 55% for TDSR and 30% for MSR. Passing either test says nothing about whether your remaining household budget is comfortable.

TDSR includes other debt obligations. MSR counts assessed property-loan repayments, including the proposed loan, for the applicable flat or EC purchase. Refinancing, exemptions and special cases need separate lender checks. MoneySense: bank LTV, cash and servicing limits.

Use the assessment rate, not only the promotional rate

For bank residential loans, the assessment uses the higher of the 4% medium-term floor and the applicable thereafter rate. A lender may use a higher rate. HDB assesses its loans at the higher of a 3% floor and the prevailing concessionary rate. The actual HDB concessionary rate is 2.6% for July–September 2026. Assessment rates determine borrowing capacity; the contracted rate determines the payment you actually make. Worked example: S$12,000 income and S$1,200 other debt

Assume the lender recognises the full S$12,000 as gross monthly income, and the S$1,200 captures all assessed other debt.

  • TDSR allows S$12,000 × 55% = S$6,600 total monthly debt service.
  • After other debt, S$6,600 − S$1,200 = S$5,400 remains for the proposed mortgage under TDSR.
  • If MSR applies, the mortgage budget is S$12,000 × 30% = S$3,600.

For an HDB flat bought with a bank loan, S$3,600 is the tighter servicing limit in this example. With a 25-year loan assessed at 4%, that supports roughly S$682,000, before the separate LTV ceiling. An HDB loan uses its own assessment rate and eligibility rules.

Turning a monthly limit into a loan amount

For monthly budget B, monthly assessment rate r, and n payments, the supported principal is B × [1 − (1 + r)−n] ÷ r. At zero interest, it is B × n. The property affordability calculator applies this calculation and then compares it with LTV.

The final loan cannot simply be the income-based result. A valuation shortfall, existing housing loans, age, tenure or remaining lease can restrict LTV. If a smaller loan creates a larger downpayment than your cash and CPF can fund, reduce the price or revisit the plan.

Inputs to confirm with the lender

Use recognised gross income rather than take-home pay for these ratios. Variable income, bonuses, self-employment earnings, joint borrowers and existing debt can receive specific underwriting treatment. Obtain the lender’s recognised-income and assessed-debt figures rather than assuming the largest income number on a payslip is fully usable.

Then run a separate household budget using take-home income. Childcare, parent support, property upkeep and savings goals affect affordability even when they are not loan instalments in a servicing ratio. Test a weaker-income month and a higher payment rate before treating the loan ceiling as a spending target.

FAQ

Does MSR apply only to HDB loans?

No. MSR also applies to bank loans for HDB flats and applicable EC purchases. Bank loans may need to pass both MSR and TDSR.

Why is my assessed payment higher than my quoted instalment?

Borrowing capacity uses an assessment interest rate, which may exceed the promotional or contracted payment rate.

Does passing TDSR make a home affordable?

No. It does not establish that your downpayment, living costs, renovation and emergency reserves are adequately funded.
