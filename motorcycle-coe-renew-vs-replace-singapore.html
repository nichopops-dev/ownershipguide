Motorcycle COE Renewal in Singapore (2026): Renew or Replace?

Renewing preserves a motorcycle whose history you know. Replacing buys a different runway but adds transaction cost and fresh uncertainty. Compare both paths over the same ownership period and include condition, downtime and end value—not just the PQP or advertised replacement price.

Quick answer

Renew when the bike passes a condition review, still fits your use and remains economical after PQP, near-term repairs and downtime are counted. Replace when renewal would lock money into a poor fit, structural or recurring faults, weak parts support, or an ownership path that is already disrupting work and safety.

Current COE renewal rules for motorcycles

LTA classifies motorcycles under Category D. The Prevailing Quota Premium (PQP) is the moving average of quota premiums from the previous three months in which bidding exercises were conducted, so it changes monthly.

RoutePaymentWhat happens later
5-year renewal50% of the applicable PQP, rounded up to the nearest dollarFor Category D, this can be done only once. The motorcycle must be deregistered after the five-year renewal ends.
10-year renewal100% of the applicable PQPA motorcycle without a statutory lifespan may be renewed again for another ten years, subject to future rules.

Under current LTA rules, motorcycles first registered before 1 July 2003 have a statutory lifespan ending on 30 June 2028. Motorcycles first registered from 1 July 2003 generally have no statutory lifespan. Check the vehicle's registration details and the live LTA rules before paying.

You must renew before expiry, or within one month after expiry with the applicable late fee. If renewal is not completed within that period, the motorcycle must be disposed of. Do not let timing make the decision for you.

Compare the same time horizon

A five-year renewal should be compared with five years of owning the replacement. A ten-year renewal should be tested over a longer horizon, with a realistic exit point if you may sell earlier. Mixing a five-year renewal payment with a replacement's ten-year headline price produces a misleading result.

Five-year ownership-cost worksheet

Renewal path = PQP paid + forgone sale or deregistration value + immediate repairs + five years of insurance, road tax and expected maintenance + downtime reserve − expected sale value at year five.

Replacement path = purchase price + financing cost + transfer and initial work + five years of insurance, road tax and expected maintenance − current-bike proceeds − expected replacement value at year five.

Divide each result by 60 for a comparable monthly ownership cost. Use ranges for repairs and resale rather than one optimistic number.

Inspect before assigning the known-bike advantage

Familiarity has value only when it is supported by evidence. Ask a competent workshop to assess the engine, transmission, cooling system, electrical charging, frame and accident signs, suspension, brakes, wheel bearings, steering, tyres and parts that are approaching their service interval. Obtain estimates for work likely within the next 12 to 24 months.

Separate ordinary wear from recurring faults. A predictable replacement of consumables is different from intermittent electrical or mechanical trouble that repeatedly creates towing, missed work or unsafe riding.

When renewal is usually stronger

  • The motorcycle has a documented service history and an inspection finds no major unresolved issue.
  • The model still fits your commute, passenger, storage and comfort needs.
  • Parts and workshop support remain practical.
  • The renewal worksheet stays competitive under a higher-repair scenario.
  • You can pay the PQP without weakening the household's emergency buffer.

When replacement is usually stronger

  • The bike has structural damage, repeated breakdowns or faults that remain difficult to diagnose.
  • Expected repairs plus downtime erase the apparent saving from renewal.
  • Your use has changed and the current motorcycle no longer fits safely or comfortably.
  • A credible replacement improves reliability or utility enough to justify its total cost.
  • Renewal depends on optimistic resale or repair assumptions to look affordable.

Choose five or ten years only after choosing renewal

The five-year route reduces the immediate PQP payment but creates a hard end point for Category D. It can suit a rider with a clear five-year use case who accepts mandatory deregistration. A ten-year renewal requires more capital but preserves a longer runway and, for motorcycles without a statutory lifespan, the possibility of another ten-year renewal later.

Model the chance that you sell early. Paying a longer renewal simply because the annualised PQP looks lower can be a poor decision if the bike is unlikely to remain suitable.

Worked comparison

Assume a five-year renewal requires S$6,000 of PQP, S$1,500 of immediate work and S$4,000 of expected maintenance above routine running cost. The current bike could otherwise produce S$2,000 of net exit value. Before insurance and road tax, the renewal commitment is S$13,500, or S$225 a month over 60 months, before subtracting any value at the end.

If a replacement requires S$18,000 net after selling the current bike, adds S$1,000 of initial work and is expected to retain S$7,000 after five years, its comparable capital cost is S$12,000 before financing and running costs. In this illustration, the replacement can be competitive even though its purchase price looks much higher. Change every input to your actual quotes.

Decision checklist

  1. Retrieve the expiry date, applicable PQP and available rebate information from OneMotoring.
  2. Obtain a written condition and near-term repair assessment.
  3. Price one realistic replacement, including financing and initial work.
  4. Model both paths over the same number of months.
  5. Stress-test renewal repairs and replacement resale value.
  6. Choose the path that remains acceptable under the less favourable case.

FAQ

Can a motorcycle COE be renewed for five years?

Yes. Pay 50% of the applicable PQP. Under current Category D rules, a five-year renewal can be used only once and the motorcycle must be deregistered when it ends.

Can a motorcycle COE be renewed again after ten years?

A motorcycle without a statutory lifespan may be renewed again in a later ten-year period, subject to the rules then in force.

When does renewal make sense?

When inspection supports the bike's condition, it still fits the rider, expected repairs and downtime are manageable, and total annual cost is competitive with a credible replacement.

What is commonly missed in the comparison?

Forgone sale and rebate value, financing, immediate repairs, downtime, end value and the fact that a Category D five-year renewal cannot be renewed again.
